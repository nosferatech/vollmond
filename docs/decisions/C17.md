## Question

§7.3 said each step of a semantic path matches a field, and fields are the members that do not begin with `$` (§5.2). Yet the
spec writes `#what-is-confirmed/$body` throughout, including in Appendix A; read literally, that address does not resolve (suite
question 18, first item). For example, `tickets/0171-x.md#done/$body` should give the prose of the section anchored `done`. Is
`#done/$title` valid too, and what about `$anchor`, `$tags` and `$key`?

## Options

- **(a) `$title`, `$body` and `$sections`.** Recommended. These three may be steps, as the spec's examples assume. `#done/$body`
  gives the prose and `#done/$sections` the list of subsections. `$anchor`, `$tags` and `$key` are reached only by exact paths
  such as `#/$sections/1/$anchor`.
- **(b) Every member of the section shape.** Chosen. `$anchor`, `$tags` and `$key` also work as steps, so `#done/$tags` returns
  the tags. More uniform. `$key` is computed, so reading it is fine but a write to it must still be refused.
- **(c) None; use flags or exact paths.** `$body` is reached only through `--body` or an exact path such as
  `#/$sections/1/$body`. The spec's examples and Appendix A change, and agents lose a form they already use.

## Decision

(b). Address steps may name every member of the section shape: `$title`, `$body`, `$sections`, `$anchor`, `$tags`, and `$key` as
a read-only computed field. Read-only computed fields are acceptable in principle, with writes to them refused. Under
[F9](F9.md), `$key` left the value view, so no computed member remains among the steps.

## Why

The owner's reason: read-only computed fields are fine and make the model self-consistent, and more read-only fields are likely
to come; if testing shows problems, it can be revisited. The card recommended (a), noting that (b) could be added later without
breaking any address that (a) allows. Why (c) lost: Not recorded.
