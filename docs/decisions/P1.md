## Question

The Markdown library slows down sharply on some valid input, and the 256-level nesting limit ([N1](N1.md)) does not bound all of
it. Measured for the Markdown parser (#79): 20,000 `*a*` in one paragraph (80 KB) take 1.5 s, 10,000 nested emphasis levels take
32 s, and 1,000 levels of list indentation take 15 s. The parser already refused a body nested deeper than 256 levels, as its
first open reading, which needed a rule. What limits bound a Markdown record?

## Options

- **(a) A resource-limit clause in §5.4 that applies to every format.** Chosen, as recommended. Nesting stays at 256 levels,
  Markdown containers included, checked before parsing; at most 4,096 runs of `*`, `_` or `~` in one paragraph or table cell; a
  record is at most 16 MiB. Beyond those, a record is a `syntax-error`.
- **(b) Keep the draft as it is.** Nesting is bounded only in data units, and the measured inputs still take a reader seconds to
  tens of seconds.

## Decision

(a), as part of card P's option (a), accepting every recommendation: nesting stays at 256 levels, Markdown containers included and
checked before parsing; at most 4,096 runs of `*`, `_` or `~` in one paragraph or table cell; a record is at most 16 MiB; beyond
those, a record is a `syntax-error`. This extends N1's nesting limit from data units to Markdown containers.

That this widens [N1](N1.md), and so supersedes it, is this record's reading; the card does not say so.

## Why

The card's reason: the limits bound hostile input far above any real file. vampiredb's busiest paragraph has 219 runs, and its
largest file is 673 KB.

Why the other options lost: Not recorded.
