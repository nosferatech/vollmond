## Question

Item 6 of card H2, from #52 Q8. The `yaml` package honors a `%YAML 1.1` directive: under it, `a: yes` gives `true`, `017` gives
15 and `2026-10-09` gives a Date (measured with `yaml` 2.9.1), while §4.4 says vmd reads YAML 1.2 core. YAML 1.2 says to read
such a document as 1.2. What does vmd do with a `%YAML 1.1` directive?

## Options

- **(a) Reject the record, with a new structural code.** Chosen, as recommended (`yaml-version-unsupported`); `%YAML 1.2` stays
  fine.
- **(b) Ignore the directive**, and read the document as YAML 1.2, as YAML 1.2 says.
- **(c) Honor it**, and read the document as YAML 1.1.

## Decision

(a), with card H2's option (a), accepting every recommendation. `%YAML 1.2` is allowed, and any other version is a structural
error with a new code, `yaml-version-unsupported`. §4.4 notes that this departs from YAML 1.2.2, section 6.8.1, twice: a 1.2
processor must accept a 1.1 document, and should process a higher minor version such as `%YAML 1.3` with a warning.

## Why

The card's reason: read as 1.2, `yes`, `017` and dates silently change meaning from what the author wrote, which is the misread
the owner wants to avoid. #52 Q8's: a silent reading under either schema, (b) or (c), surprises someone. The log's: reading a
`%YAML 1.1` record as 1.2 would silently change what `yes` or `017` mean to its author. When applying it, the session kept the
rejection although it departs from the YAML standard, since reading such documents as 1.2 is the silent misread to avoid. Card
H2's reason for accepting every recommendation: each keeps vmd strict and its output readable back, and none contradicts an
earlier decision.
