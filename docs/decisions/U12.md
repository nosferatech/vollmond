## Question

If a collection's schema cannot be used, either because it is broken or because it uses a construct from a newer minor version
(an unknown `x-vmd-*` keyword), what do reads do? The question matters for versioning: [U21](U21.md) depends on it.

## Options

- **(a) Report it; readers refuse its records.** Chosen, as recommended. `check` reports `schema-invalid` once, goes on with the
  other collections, and checks that collection's records for everything but their schema. `get`, `outline` and `resolve` on one
  of its records fail with `schema-invalid`. A query leaves them out, as it leaves out records with structural errors, and
  returns the other collections' matches with one issue counting what it left out; it fails as a whole only in the strict read
  mode, where refused records are errors. `ls` counts them unreadable, and a reference with a fragment into one is `unreadable`.
  This reverses [F1](F1.md) (C2 as F1 narrowed it) and the Draft v0.4 correction "Whether a record is readable never depends on
  its schema". §5.4, §9.5, §8.5, J3's rule and Appendix D change.
- **(b) Report it; reads go on without the schema.** What the first version of the design proposed. An older reader would read a
  newer store's collection unschematized: typed references and dates silently become plain strings. That is a misread, not a
  refusal.
- **(c) Every command fails.** Every command that opens the store fails, `check` and the collections whose schemas are fine
  included: one broken schema hides the whole store.

## Decision

(a). This reverses recorded decisions, and the card put it as such: [F1](F1.md)'s rule, from C2 as F1 narrowed it, that only
structural errors make a record unreadable, and the correction of Draft v0.4, "Whether a record is readable never depends on its
schema", which stays in the decision log and has no record of its own. Under (a), a record without a structural error is still
refused by readers when its collection's schema cannot be used. It also widens [J3](J3.md): the status `unreadable`, and
`ref-target-unreadable`, cover a reference into a record of a collection with an invalid schema, as well as one into a record with
a structural error. §5.4's and §9.5's readability gain "provided the collection's schema can be used", and the count of records
`ls` and `query` could not read includes them.

## Why

The card's reason: "It is what makes your versioning rule hold (an older reader refuses rather than misreads); (b) misreads
silently, and (c) hides the whole store over one schema. I'm recommending a reversal of your earlier decision knowingly; if you'd
rather keep C2/F1, (b) is the alternative and U21 must then be (b) too." The reason is F1's own, read the other way: a reader must
not return a value it would read wrongly, and without its schema it would. The design adds that (a) refuses only the affected
collection's records, and only to readers, where (c) refuses everything, and that U21's (a) depends on this answer, since it is
what keeps every reader from returning data under a schema the reader cannot use.
