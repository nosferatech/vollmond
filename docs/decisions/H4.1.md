## Question

§7.5 said "the shortest form" but listed its rules in order. For `o: {$anchor: owner-of-record}`, is the canonical address of
`/o/name` `#owner-of-record/name` or `#o/name`? And is the root's address `""` even when its title heading has
`<a id="release">`, so that a root field is `#status` rather than `#release/status`? The address fixtures (#53, questions 3, 4,
6 and 7) also asked about a derived anchor whose address is ambiguous and a duplicated explicit anchor.

## Options

- **(a) The rules in order, the first that gives a singular address; the root is always `""`, so its field is `#status`.**
  Chosen, as recommended. An anchor longer than the root path wins (Q4), since it survives a move; a derived anchor whose
  address is ambiguous falls through to the exact path (Q6), and so does a duplicated explicit anchor (Q7).
- **(b) The shortest form,** as §7.5 worded it: `#o/name` for `/o/name`.
- **(c) The root counts as an anchored ancestor,** so the root is `#release` and its field `#release/status`.

When the owner confirmed the order after the review of #57, the question offered two orders:

- **Most stable first.** Chosen, as recommended. Explicit anchor, then semantic path, then exact path as the last resort. A
  canonical address keeps its meaning when sections are inserted.
- **Exact path first.** The canonical address is always the exact path: precise, but it changes whenever a section is inserted
  above.

## Decision

(a), as card H4's option (a), accepting every recommendation. The first form that gives a singular address for the node wins,
not the shortest. The root's canonical address is always `""`, even when its title heading has an anchor, so a root field is
`#status`. §7.5 makes the order explicit and gives the reason: the most stable form comes first (an explicit anchor), and the
exact path, the most precise form but the one an insertion breaks, is the last resort. §7.5 now lists four forms (explicit
anchor, path from the nearest anchored ancestor or the root, derived anchor, exact path) where Draft v0.4 had three rules. The
conformance README's open question 1 is closed. Asked again after the review of this round, the owner confirmed the order: most
stable first.

## Why

The PR's reasons: the anchor survives a move (Q4); the derived anchor must resolve as a singular address, not merely be a unique
anchor (Q6); a duplicated anchor is not singular (Q7); and the root never counting as an anchored ancestor keeps its fields at
`#status` (Q3). In the confirmation, the exact path first lost because it changes whenever a section is inserted above. The
card's note for the six items: each recommendation keeps vmd strict and its output readable back, and none contradicts an earlier
decision.
