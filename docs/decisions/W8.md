## Question

The card: "Pull requests #130, #136 and #147 and Draft v0.16 filled in these details. Each is labelled "Agent's reading, for the
owner to confirm". Confirming makes them decided; any you reject becomes its own card." The card lists eight; the records that
hold them are added in parentheses:

1. **`type: 5` is a broken schema, not a newer one.** The card: "A non-string value of `type`, `cardinality` or `anchors` in vmd's
   schema keywords is the wrong form (`schema-invalid`), while an unknown string such as `type: set` says it may need a newer vmd
   (AA2, #130)." ([AA2](AA2.md))
2. **An array position closed by `false` holds nothing.** The card: "Past `items: false`, or at a `prefixItems` entry `false`, no
   value can stand, so nothing meets there and no format is needed through it. Only the literal `false` counts; a schema that
   happens to admit nothing, like `{not: {}}`, doesn't (AA4, #136)." ([AA4](AA4.md))
3. **A target matched both by an explicit anchor and by its title counts as explicit.** The card: "`## Notes <a id="notes"></a>`
   reached by `#notes` is allowed under `anchors: explicit` (AM)." ([AM](AM.md), reading 2)
4. **Both title warnings apply to many-valued references.** The card: "`ref-derived-repeat` takes `ref-derived-anchor`'s place
   when the title repeats, as for single links (AM). How far they reach is card AQ." (AM, reading 3; [AQ](AQ.md))
5. **A declaration on a `$ref` member follows the usual rules.** The card: "it still needs `format: uri-reference` beside it, and
   is read only along the schema's spine (AN)." ([AN](AN.md))
6. **Any anchor counts as a link's single match.** The card: "In a repeating journal, a derived anchor that names one section
   (`#monday-1`, the second Monday) or a block anchor (`<a id="desk"></a>` in a paragraph) is singular, as an explicit anchor is
   (AO, #147)." ([AO](AO.md), reading 2; #147's readings 2 and 3)
7. **A refused link's message names the places it found.** The card: "("names 2 nodes in the record, /$sections/0 and …"), but no
   candidate targets are attached to it, as for any link the check can't prove (#147)." (#147's reading 4)
8. **What "through a repeating level" means in U3.** The card: "(the rule that such links are `ambiguous`): a step the proof
   doesn't accept. A first step that names exactly one node isn't "through" the root's level (AO, U3)." ([U3](U3.md); AO,
   reading 3)

## Options

- **(a) Confirm all eight.** Chosen, as recommended. The card: "The labels come off in the next draft. Nothing in the code
  changes."
- **(b) Confirm, except what the note says.** The card: "Each exception becomes its own card."
- **(c) Discuss first.** The card: "The readings stay labelled."

## Decision

(a): all eight readings are confirmed. The records that hold them, AA2, AA4, AM (readings 2 and 3), AN, AO (readings 2 and 3, the
latter for its U3 part) and U3, say so. Items 6 and 7, from #147's description, had no record; Draft v0.17 states them in §7.4,
§8.5 and Appendix D's `ref-ambiguous` row. AM's reading 1, how Draft v0.16 chose §9.3's example, and AO's reading that AO
supersedes AD are not on the card.

## Why

The card's reason: "Each is what the merged code does and follows the safe side or the existing rule for single links. None has a
cost a user would notice beyond the rule it details."

Why (b) and (c) lost: Not recorded.

## Owner's note

The project owner chose (a) on the review desk on 2026-10-20, with no note.
