## Question

§9.1 (collections), §10 (`@path`) and §11.2 (`list`, `grep`) all take globs, with only `*` and `**` defined. The storage
contract and filesystem backend (#68) noted that glob syntax beyond them (`?`, `[...]`, `{a,b}`) is undefined, and matched those
characters as themselves meanwhile. What is the glob syntax?

## Options

- **(a) One syntax for all three:** `*` matches within one path segment, dotfiles included; `**` matches any depth; every other
  character matches itself. Chosen, as recommended.

Other options: Not recorded.

## Decision

(a), with card M's option (a), accepting every recommendation. One glob syntax for a collection's `match` and `exclude` (§9.1),
the configuration's `ignore` (§3.3), VQL's `@path` (§10.3) and `list` and `grep` (§11.2): `*` within one segment, dotfiles
included, `**` any depth, and every other character matches itself. It is defined once, in §11.2. Whether `docs/**` matches
`docs` itself was left open by the card; the draft follows the filesystem backend's `compileGlob`, which requires `docs/`.

## Why

The card's reason for the card as a whole: each rule gives one meaning in every language and engine, which is what portable is
for.

Why the other options lost: Not recorded.

## Applied in

Draft v0.9 (#73, `75c8728`), §11.2, with §3.3, §9.1 and §10.3 pointing to it. The storage review (#16) then gave the globs their
wording in Draft v0.10, without a decision: `**/` matches zero or more whole segments with their `/`, a final `/**` matches one or
more, and a malformed glob is `query-invalid`.
