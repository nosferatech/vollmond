## Question

On [C22](C22.md) the owner asked whether aliases are needed, since `mv` rewrites every reference. Within one commit, it does;
aliases cover what `mv` cannot see. A branch or pull request opened before the move adds a new link to the old anchor; it merges
without a textual conflict, because the lines differ, and the link then dangles on main. And links outside the store: bookmarks,
other repositories, the website's published URLs. With an alias, the old address still resolves (reported as `aliased`), and
`vmd check --fix` rewrites it; without one, CI flags it after the merge and someone repairs it by hand. The spec then wrote an
alias only with `--keep-alias`, or when the target is referenced from outside, which misses the branch case. When are aliases
written?

## Options

- **(a) Write aliases on every move, prune them later.** Chosen, as recommended. `mv` and `rename` always record an alias.
  `vmd alias prune` removes those no open branch can still need. In-flight branches heal by themselves, and the alias file grows
  until pruned.
- **(b) Keep the current rule (`--keep-alias`).** No growth, but references from branches older than the move dangle after
  merging and are fixed by hand.
- **(c) Drop aliases.** Simplest. Published URLs and in-flight branches break on every move, so the website needs its own
  redirects.

## Decision

(a). `mv` and `rename` record an alias on every move, and `vmd alias prune` removes those no open branch can still need. The
owner's caveat is to be resolved in the design (#48): a reference to `old-anchor` committed before the rename is meant for the
renamed node, but one committed after the rename may mean a new anchor that reuses the name, and a client synced before the
rename that pushes after it is indistinguishable from one that intends the new meaning.

## Why

The card's reason: concurrent branches are the normal case with several agents at work. The owner's: keep them for now, since
any backend store needs to see the list of renames when a commit happens anyway. The card's consequences for the other options,
not reasons it gives against them: (b) leaves references from older branches dangling; (c) breaks published URLs and in-flight
branches on every move.

## Owner's note

> Lets keep it for now. Any backend store will need to see the list of renames when commit happens anyways, even a store that is
> capable of detecting add vs rename conflict that you described.
> However care will need to be take here:
> When add of "old-anchor" is commited before rename "old-anchor" to "new-anchor", then the old-anchor should be considered to be
> new-anchor and should be fixed.
> When add of "old-anchor" is commited after rename "old-anchor" to "new-anchor", then the added "old-anchor" has nothing to do
> with the rename.  This brings a problem, of detecting and correcting issues on client side that adds the "old-anchor", if a
> client is synced to commit from before rename, adds the "old-anchor" and then pushes commit, this will be indistinguishable from
> the case, were client intends to add "old-anchor" normally after the rename.
> Is there a way to fix that?
