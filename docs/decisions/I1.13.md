## Question

Item 13 of card I1, from #57's open questions: RFC 3339, section 5.7, allows `:60` only at a real leap second, which needs a table
of leap seconds, and §4.3 did not say whether the `date-time` assertion applies the restriction. What does a date-time accept?

## Options

- **(a) Accept `:60` in any date-time, and do not check a table.** Chosen, as recommended; §4.3 says so.
- **(b) Apply RFC 3339's restriction, `:60` only at a real leap second**, which needs a table of leap seconds.

## Decision

Card I1's option (a), accepting every recommendation: a date-time accepts `:60` anywhere, with no leap-second table (§4.3). The
decision stands as the owner's, with its reason corrected.

## Why

The card's reason: "as JSON Schema validators commonly do". The log's correction: that reason was checked and does not hold.
Ajv's `ajv-formats` accepts `:60` only at 23:59 UTC, and Python's `jsonschema` checks `date-time` only when `rfc3339-validator`
is installed, which rejects `:60`. `ajv-formats` also accepts a space separator and the offsets `+02` and `+0200`, which §4.3
rejects, so vmd needs its own date and time checks (Appendix C, task I2.2).

Why the other options lost: Not recorded.

## Applied in

Draft v0.6 (#58, `c3879b7`), §4.3 and Appendix C. Replaced in Draft v0.7 by [J1](J1.md): `:60` only at 23:59 UTC.
