## Question

Item 1 of card J, from the independent review of #58. The owner chose [I1.13](I1.13.md), "accept `:60` anywhere", partly on the
session's claim that JSON Schema validators do the same. That was wrong: Ajv's `ajv-formats`, which the plan uses, accepts `:60`
only at 23:59 UTC, and Python's `jsonschema` rejects `:60` outright (both checked in their source). Where may a seconds field of
`60` stand?

## Options

- **(a) `:60` anywhere, as decided in I1.13.**
- **(b) `:60` only at 23:59 UTC, as Ajv does, still with no leap-second table.** Chosen, as recommended.

## Decision

(b), through card J's option (a), accepting every recommendation. A seconds field of `60` is accepted only at 23:59 UTC, after the
offset is applied, as Ajv's `ajv-formats` does, with no table of leap seconds. For ordering, `23:59:60` falls between `23:59:59`
and the next midnight (§4.3). That a local date-time, which has no offset, is taken as UTC for this check, as `ajv-formats` takes
a time without an offset, is the agent's reading, as the log records; card [W12](W12.md) put it to the owner, and it
became card [AV](AV.md), which decided otherwise.

## Why

The card's reason: (b) catches `12:30:60`, which is almost always a typo, and agrees with the main JavaScript validator. Either
way, vmd needs its own date-time check, since Ajv also accepts a space separator and `+02` offsets, which §4.3 forbids. The log's
reason for replacing I1.13: I1.13's reason, given by the session that drafted the card, was wrong, since `ajv-formats` restricts
`:60` to 23:59 UTC and `rfc3339-validator` rejects it. For the whole card: each recommendation "keeps one rule per thing and
matches what the libraries vmd builds on can check".

## Applied in

Draft v0.7 (#61, `c3032a6`), §4.3.

Draft v0.19: [AV](AV.md) decides the reading above the other way: a local date-time accepts `:60` in any minute, as the grammar
allows, since without a zone any narrower rule guesses the zone. This record is superseded for local date-times; its rule and its
reason stand for a `time` and a `date-time`, which have an offset.
