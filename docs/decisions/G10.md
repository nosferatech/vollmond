## Question

[G4](G4.md) forbids title-derived steps under `anchors: explicit`, and the author of #50 asked whether exact paths are then
allowed. Exact paths include positions, like `#/$sections/2`, which point at a different section once one is inserted above it:
the kind of silent retargeting the setting exists to prevent, by position instead of by title. Under `anchors: explicit`, may a
reference use positions?

## Options

- **(a) No positional steps (section or list indexes).** Chosen, as recommended. Allowed: explicit anchors, field names,
  keyed-list keys. `#done/notes`, where `notes` is a field, is fine; `#/$sections/2` and `#done/items/0` are rejected. Inserting
  a section or an item never changes what a reference with the setting means.
- **(b) Allow exact paths, as #50 had it.** Simpler. A reference still breaks silently when a section or list item is inserted
  above its target.

## Decision

(a). Under `anchors: explicit` (§9.3), a reference may not use positional steps, section or list indexes, in a semantic path or
an exact path. Explicit anchors, field names and keyed-list keys are allowed. Draft v0.4 allowed every exact path under
`anchors: explicit`; G10 narrows that (§9.3, Appendix D `ref-target-not-allowed`). Other references may use positions at their
own risk.

## Why

The card's reason: the setting promises a reference that keeps its meaning, and positions break that just as titles do. (b) keeps
the simpler rule at the cost of a reference that breaks silently when something is inserted above its target.

## Owner's note

> When using a strict reference we should prohibit index paths. But regular anchors can use them at their own risk.
