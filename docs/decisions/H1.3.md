## Question

Item 3 of card H1, from #52 Q4 and #53 Q13. How many issues, and where: for a member name repeated three times
(`{"a": 1, "a": 2, "a": 3}`), three nodes on one anchor, a heading whose explicit and derived anchors both collide with an
earlier block anchor, two unpaired surrogates in one string (`"\udc00\ud800"`), and an unpaired surrogate in a member name
(`{"\ud800": 1}`), whose exact path would itself hold a lone surrogate?

## Options

- **(a) One issue per occurrence after the first; one `unpaired-surrogate` per string, at the string, and at the object for one
  in a member name.** Chosen, as recommended: two `duplicate-member` for a name given three times, two `duplicate-anchor` for
  three nodes on one anchor.
- **(b) The other counts #52 Q4 and #53 Q13 name.** One `duplicate-member` for the repeated name; one `unpaired-surrogate` per
  surrogate rather than per string; for a heading colliding through both its anchors, two issues rather than one; for three nodes
  on one anchor, an issue for the second only.

## Decision

(a), with card H1's option (a), accepting every recommendation. A name given three times gives two `duplicate-member`, and three
nodes with one anchor give two `duplicate-anchor`, one per node, so a heading whose explicit and derived anchors both collide with
an earlier block anchor gets one. `unpaired-surrogate` is reported once per string, at the string, and for a surrogate in a
member name at the object that holds the member. Appendix D states the counting rule once, in its introduction, and applies it
also to `duplicate-key` and `path-case-conflict`, whose "the second" had the same gap; that extension is the agent's reading of
H1.3's general wording.

## Why

The reasons in #52 Q4: one `unpaired-surrogate` per string, since Appendix D says "a string holding an unpaired surrogate"; a
surrogate in a member name attaches to the object, as `yaml-non-string-key` is attached to the mapping. Card H1's reason for
accepting every recommendation: each keeps vmd strict and its output readable back, and none contradicts an earlier decision.
Why the other options lost: Not recorded.
