## Question

What is a front matter delimiter, and what is front matter with no closing line? The Markdown fixtures (#54, question 4) asked
with front matter that is never closed, `--- ` with a trailing space, and an indented `---` inside a block scalar. A file with no
closing line could be plain Markdown that opens with a thematic break, or a structural error. §5.3 also claimed that front
matter works "as Jekyll uses it", which needed a source or had to go.

## Options

- **(a) A delimiter is exactly `---` at the start of a line, optionally followed by spaces or tabs, and front matter with no
  closing line is a structural error** (`syntax-error` at the root, or a new code). Chosen, as recommended. `--- ` delimits, and
  an indented `---` in a block scalar does not.
- **(b) Read unclosed front matter as Markdown** that opens with a thematic break, so the fields become prose.

## Decision

(a), as card H3's option (a), accepting every recommendation. A delimiter is exactly `---` at the start of a line, optionally
followed by spaces or tabs. Unclosed front matter is a structural error; the answer named no code, so the draft uses
`syntax-error` at the root, the first of the two the recommendation offered. The claim about Jekyll was checked: Jekyll's
`YAML_FRONT_MATTER_REGEXP` also accepts `...` as the closing line, so §5.3 now says that vmd's form is narrower, and cites
Jekyll's source and GitHub's announcement of front matter tables.

## Why

The PR's reason: otherwise a missing closing line silently turns the fields into prose, which is what (b) does. The card's note
for all sixteen items: each recommendation keeps vmd strict and its output readable back, and none contradicts an earlier
decision.
