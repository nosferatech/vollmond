## Question

Item 1 of card Y, from the pull request that implements cards P, Q, R and T (#94) and its independent review, which found
nothing blocking. [P1](P1.md)'s limits work as decided: nesting stops at 256 levels, a paragraph at 4,096 runs and a record at
16 MiB, and the three inputs on card P went from 46 s, 24 s and 2.7 s to under 0.15 s each. But the review measured records that
are valid under P1 and still slow, because the time goes into inline parsing after the limits are checked: 1,000 paragraphs, each
with 256 nested emphasis levels (1.5 MB), take 12.7 s; 4,000 copies of `*`×256 `a` `*`×256 (2 MB) take 7.9 s; 40 copies of
`*`×2000 `a` `*`×2000 (160 KB) take 3.6 s. Some slow inputs need no emphasis at all, and are as slow on main: 20,000 nested link
brackets take 20.7 s, and 2,000 code spans of different backtick lengths take 32 s. So §5.4's "a reader's time is bounded" is
not true yet; all of it is on #88.

The pull request also adds a 256-level limit on inline nesting, which the draft does not have. Without it, one long run can nest
5,000 levels and overflow the stack. It is recorded as a deviation on #13, and it refuses a record the draft calls readable only
beyond 256 levels of emphasis or links. What does P1 promise?

## Options

- **(a) Make inline nesting part of the 256 limit, which bounds the stack on every machine, and reword §5.4 so that it does not
  promise bounded time.** Chosen, as recommended. The worst case, roughly 5 to 8 s per MB, is treated as a performance bug for
  #88, which has the research on lazy inline parsing; #94's inline limit stops being a deviation.
- **A per-record time budget.** Rejected by the card: "the outcome would depend on the machine, so the conformance suite couldn't
  test it."

Card Y's options covered its three items together: (a) "Accept all three"; (b) "Accept, except what the note says", under which
the exceptions keep today's behaviour or get their own card; and (c) "Discuss first", under which #94 lands as is, the inline
limit stays a recorded deviation, and the owner and the session talk about limits.

## Decision

(a), as part of card Y's option (a), accepting all three: inline nesting is part of the 256-level limit, §5.4 promises a bounded
stack, not bounded time, and the worst case is a performance bug tracked on #88.

**Agent's reading, for the owner to confirm.** Two details the card left open, which Draft v0.12 states as #94 implements them:
inline nesting is counted apart from the containers, within each block or data `$title`, an element at the top counting as the
first level, so 256 levels of emphasis inside a block quote are within the limit; and the inline elements that count are
emphasis, strong emphasis, strikethrough and links (reference links included), where the card named "emphasis or links". That
this amends P1, and so supersedes it, is also this record's reading; the card does not say so.

## Why

The card's reason: "It makes P1's promise true (stack, not time), closes two cheap gaps, and leaves the real time bound to #88's
parser research instead of pretending." Without the inline limit, one long run can nest 5,000 levels and overflow the stack.

Why a per-record time budget lost: its outcome would depend on the machine, so the conformance suite could not test it. Why (b)
and (c) of the card lost: Not recorded.
