## Question

Item 7 of card H2, from #52 Q9. What does a logical type's `format` assertion accept: only the type's canonical form, or every
form its standard allows? Examples that differ: `+00:00` against `Z`, a lower-case `t`, `PT1.5S`, int64 as the string `"42"`,
decimal `".5"`. #52's cases used only values valid, or invalid, under both readings.

## Options

- **(a) Everything the referenced standard allows.** Chosen, as recommended. §4.3 then spells out int64, bigint, decimal and
  local-date-time, and pins duration to RFC 3339 Appendix A, which JSON Schema validators implement. The writer still emits the
  canonical form.
- **(b) Only the canonical form**, the other reading #52 Q9 names.

## Decision

(a), with card H2's option (a), accepting every recommendation. A `format` assertion accepts every form of the standard the type
refers to, not only the canonical form, and the writer emits the canonical form. §4.3 gives each type's lexical space, spells out
those of int64, bigint, decimal and local-date-time, and pins duration to RFC 3339 Appendix A. One consequence is the agent's:
local date-times compare chronologically rather than as strings, since `T`, `t` and a space give the same value. Two follow-up
questions went to the owner: [H2.7a](H2.7a.md), decimal's lexical space, and [H2.7b](H2.7b.md), a space between date and time.

## Why

Card H2's reason for accepting every recommendation: each keeps vmd strict and its output readable back, and none contradicts an
earlier decision. No reason specific to this item is recorded. Why (b) lost: Not recorded.
