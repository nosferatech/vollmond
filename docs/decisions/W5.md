## Question

Draft v0.14 (#105) applies the owner's answers to cards AA2 to AA4 (schema checking) and AB (link definitions shared across a
record's sections). The card: "Where your answers on AA2–AA4 (schema checking) and AB (link definitions shared across a record's
sections) left a detail open, Draft v0.14's author decided it and labelled it in the records as "Agent's reading, for the owner
to confirm". Confirming makes them decided; any you reject becomes its own card." The card lists ten:

1. **Recursive schemas can't declare in their recursive part** ([AA3](AA3.md)). The card: "In the standard recursive-tree
   schema, with `$dynamicAnchor` on the root, every part is reachable through `$dynamicRef`, so any `x-vmd-list` or `x-vmd-ref`
   anywhere makes the schema `schema-invalid`. With the tree moved into `$defs`, the root can declare and the tree's nodes can't.
   Recursion still works; vmd's keywords just can't be used inside it."
2. **Also when reached another way** (AA3 with [AA4](AA4.md) item 6): a vmd keyword at a `$dynamicAnchor` is refused even when
   the schema also reaches it normally.
3. **AA3's explanation** of where `$dynamicRef` can land sits in §9.2.
4. **Wrong form vs unknown value** ([AA2](AA2.md)): only a value outside a member's defined set is `feature-unsupported`; a value
   of the wrong form, such as `keys: "name"` or a malformed glob, stays `schema-invalid`.
5. **Two keywords outside AA2**: `x-vmd-summary` and `x-vmd-ordered` with a bad value stay `schema-invalid`.
6. **Record links**: AA2 marks [W1](W1.md) as superseded, and AA4 marks [U2](U2.md) as superseded.
7. **"Apply together" in words** (AA4): the draft spells out when two parts of a schema apply to the same value, as #97's code
   works; #97 misses the case of a `prefixItems` entry against a keyed item's schema, which #109 fixes.
8. **A typed reference needs its format on every path** (AA4 item 2), as #97 implements it.
9. **Which definition wins** ([AB](AB.md)): "first" is in the order of the record's Markdown form, a section's `$body` before its
   subsections', whatever order the keys have in the JSON or YAML file.
10. **Where AB is stated**: once, in §5.4; §5.5 and §8.1 point at it.

## Options

- **(a) Confirm all ten.** Chosen, as recommended. The card: "The labels go in the next draft. Nothing in the code changes."
- **(b) Confirm, except what the note says.** The card: "Each exception becomes its own card."
- **(c) Discuss first.** The card: "The readings stay labelled."

## Decision

(a): all ten readings are confirmed. The records that hold them, AA2, AA3, AA4 and AB, say so. The owner first chose (b), asking
for an example of items 1 and 2; the engineering manager gave them in chat (a standard extensible recursive tree with
`$dynamicAnchor` on the root, and the same tree moved into `$defs`), and the owner then confirmed all ten.

## Why

The card's reason: "Each follows the safe side of your answers or what the code already does. Item 1 is the one with a real cost
to users: reject it if vmd's keywords should work inside recursive schemas, and it becomes its own card." AA3's note stands: "If
it causes trouble, we can disable it before 1.0".

Why (b) and (c) lost: Not recorded.
