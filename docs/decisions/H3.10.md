## Question

How is an info string close to `yaml data` read: `yaml  data` (two spaces), `yaml` and a tab and `data`, `yaml data strict` (a
third word), `YAML data` and `Yaml Data` (letter case), and `yaml&#32;data`, whose info string is `yaml data` only after
CommonMark (section 4.5) decodes references and escapes in it? The Markdown fixtures (#54, question 9) listed the readings: the
whole info string compared, or split into words; case-sensitive or not; the info string as written or as CommonMark processes it;
and a third word ignored, making the fence prose, or `feature-unsupported`.

## Options

- **(a) Split on spaces and tabs, compare case-sensitively, on the source text; a third word is `feature-unsupported`.**
  Chosen, as recommended. Exactly the two words `yaml data` or `json data` make a data block, and `yaml&#32;data` is prose.
- **(b) Compare the whole info string with `yaml data`.**
- **(c) Compare without regard to letter case,** which would flip the case `data-blocks/first-word-is-exact`.
- **(d) Compare the info string as CommonMark processes it,** after decoding references and escapes.
- **(e) A third word ignored, or making the fence prose.**

## Decision

(a), as card H3's option (a), accepting every recommendation. Info strings are split on spaces and tabs and compared
case-sensitively on the source text, before CommonMark's decoding (CommonMark 0.31.2, section 4.5). A third word is
`feature-unsupported`.

## Why

The PR's reasons: a third word is `feature-unsupported` so that options added later stay detectable (§9.1), where ignoring it or
reading prose would not; comparing the source text makes the marker what the author typed, and an implementation need not decode
references before deciding. Why (b) and (c) lost: Not recorded. The card's note for all sixteen items: each recommendation keeps
vmd strict and its output readable back, and none contradicts an earlier decision.
