## Question

A singular address must hit exactly one node, as every reference and write target must. §7.4 said such an address is valid only
if each step crosses a level whose keys are unique, and that the checker proves this before evaluating anything. Two cases escape
that proof (suite question 8): in lenient mode duplicates are allowed, so `#notes` may be unique today and ambiguous once someone
adds a second `## Notes`; and in any mode the first step can match a root field and also another node's anchor, for example the
front-matter field `done: true` and a heading anchored `done`. Only evaluation sees both. May a singular address still fail as
ambiguous at evaluation?

## Options

- **(a) Yes, in exactly those two cases.** Chosen, as recommended. §7.4 says the proof covers the levels an address crosses, and
  that a singular address can still fail as ambiguous at evaluation in these two cases. `vmd check` reports such references as
  ambiguous and writes refuse them. Authors face no new restriction.
- **(b) No; forbid both cases in advance.** A root field whose name equals any anchor in the record becomes a duplicate, and
  lenient mode refuses singular addresses through levels no schema declares unique. Every singular address is then proven before
  use, but front matter `done:` next to an anchor `done` becomes an error, and lenient stores lose most of their semantic
  references.

## Decision

(a). A singular address may still fail as ambiguous when evaluated, in exactly two cases, lenient-mode duplicates and a first
step that matches both a root member and another node's anchor. §7.4 says so.

## Why

The card's reason: as the suite recommends; (b)'s restrictions cost authors more than the rare evaluation-time failure costs
anyone.

## Applied in

Draft v0.4 (#50, `1043a6c`), §7.4. [H4.2](H4.2.md) (Draft v0.5) added a third case: duplicates in strict data that break the
uniqueness the proof relies on.
